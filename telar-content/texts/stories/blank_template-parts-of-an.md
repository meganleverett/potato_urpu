---
title: "Parts of an Urpu"
---

 The Urpu was constructed using a combination of handbuilding techniques, including coil building for the main body and the implementation of press molds for the trumpet-flared lip. Additionally, the conical base is also constructed separately and is adhered after the trumpet-flared lip. To attach the components, their surface is scratched to be roughly textured (scored), and a watery clay mixture called slip is added to function like glue. The mouth and body of the vessel are slipped and scored together first, and then the vessel is flipped to stand on its head. At this point, the conical base is attached. The adhesion of all parts, including handles and lugs, is completed at the plastic phase in the clay's drying process, while the clay is still workable before it reaches the leather hard phase, where any further structural working of the clay beyond carving and decoration would lead to cracking and a scrapped piece.

Image translation:

"An ovoid vessel is constructed (using the coil-and-paddle technique). The mouth of the urpu is built using the coiling technique. The vessels are created and then left to dry slightly so that they can be handled.
Finally, the vessel is closed in a conical shape using the coiling technique. A hole is cut into the base of the ovoid vessel, and the mouth is then joined to the body (smoothed and sealed before the vessel is closed).
Vessel is finished.

**Process of lifting the body of the vessel:** once it has air-dried, the mouth of the urpu is inserted.
**The process of raising the mouth of the urpu:** the walls of the cylindrical body are raised, after which coiled clay is added until the desired height is achieved. The surface is then smoothed, and the mouth is formed using a cloth."
